Reading a candle

Every candle on the chart above is one minute of BTC price. The body is the range between open and close; the wicks show the high and low the price touched along the way.

Green (bullish)

Close finished higher than open — buyers were in control for that minute. A string of these with small wicks is a steady uptrend.

Red (bearish)

Close finished lower than open — sellers won that minute. Long red bodies after a rally often mark the start of a pullback.

Wicks

A long wick with a small body means price was rejected — it tried to go further and got pushed back. That's often how the model flags support or resistance.

What the lines on the chart mean

These are the exact overlays your bot draws — anchor VWAP, deviation bands, exhaustion markers, support/resistance, and the strike line.

Anchor VWAP

The volume-weighted average price for the current 15-min window, resetting to zero at the start of every window (that's the "anchor"). It's the model's sense of where price has "really" traded this window, not just where it is right now.

Deviation bands

The two faded green lines above and below the VWAP are ±1 and ±2 standard deviations of price around it. Price sitting near the outer band means the move is stretched — historically more likely to snap back than keep running.

Exhaustion (exh)

A purple arrow marks a candle whose wick poked past the outer band but closed back inside it, with the wick bigger than the body — a classic sign buyers or sellers pushed too far and got rejected. Often an early reversal warning.

Support / Resistance

Flat lines held at the most recent confirmed swing high/low. Price tends to react (bounce or break) when it revisits one of these — the model tracks how many times a level gets "touched" and whether it holds.

Strike (Kalshi)

The dashed amber line is Kalshi's actual strike for the current contract, pulled directly from Kalshi's public market data (no account needed) — not an estimate. If that fetch ever fails, it falls back to the model's own window-open price as a close approximation, and the HUD tells you which one you're looking at.

RSI panel

The purple line pinned to the bottom of the chart is RSI over time — the same series the model reads for momentum, just visible instead of a single number.

●/● Prediction label

Once per 15-min round, a small green "UP" or red "DOWN" chip appears above the candle where the model made its first call for that whole round — not a per-minute signal, just the one prediction that round's outcome gets graded against in the track record tab.

⚠ Exit / flip warning

An amber dot marks the exact bar where the reversal check first flags the held call as at risk of flipping. Your bot's "Confirmed" label deliberately never changes mid-round once locked (see the Signal Tiers tab) — this marker is the closest thing to a live "it might be about to go the other way" signal.

■ Round end

A small square marks the exact bar where each round closed, colored by outcome — green if the first call won, red if it lost, gray if no call fired that round.

Pattern scanner lines

Two amber trendlines mark a bull/bear flag when one's forming — the "pole" is the sharp move, the channel between the lines is the pause after it. A circle marks a double top or double bottom, with a "neckline" price. Both come from checking real trendline math against the last hour of bars, not guesswork — they were tested against random price noise to keep false alarms rare, so if you see one, it cleared a real bar.

How the model calls a signal

The engine doesn't just guess — it runs the live price through momentum, volatility, and support/resistance checks, then only "commits" once enough of them agree. Weakest to strongest:
Value Zone — the earliest heads-up. Model probability just crossed into a lean, contract is usually still cheap.
Early Pick — momentum accelerating in one direction for a couple of bars in a row, with a real-bodied candle behind it.
Early Confirm — Early Pick's conditions held up longer and probability cleared a higher bar. Meaningfully more reliable.
Confirmed — the strongest tier: trend, momentum, and probability all agree over multiple bars. Highest confidence, fires latest.

Why the pick doesn't always match "Settle %"

Once a tier fires, it locks to that direction for the rest of the 15-min round on purpose — so the badge doesn't flip-flop every second as price wiggles. If the raw model probability later swings hard the other way, the pick badge above will show a caution warning instead of silently flipping, and the "Reversal chance" HUD row is exactly this same check, expressed as a %.

The raw indicators panel

These are the numbers in the "Raw indicators" section of the HUD — the actual inputs the model's tiers are built from.

RSI

Relative Strength Index — measures whether recent buying or selling pressure has dominated. Above 70 = overbought (buying pressure stretched), below 30 = oversold.

EMA fast / slow

Two moving averages at different speeds. When the fast one crosses above the slow one, that's a bullish momentum shift; crossing below is bearish.

Volatility (σ)

How much price has been swinging recently, measured on log returns. Higher volatility widens the model's uncertainty and tightens its thresholds.

ROC / Accel

Rate of change over the last 3 bars, and how much that rate is itself speeding up or slowing down — the model's way of catching momentum before it's obvious.

Body strength

How much of a candle's full range is "body" (open-to-close) vs. wick. A strong body means conviction; a weak one (mostly wick) means indecision.

Calibrated %

Not the model's raw stated probability — its real historical win rate for calls at this confidence level, so far. If the model says "70%" but those calls have actually won 61% of the time, this shows 61%.

Market regimes

The same signal means different things depending on what kind of market this is. The model classifies the current regime and adjusts how strict it is accordingly.

Trending

Price is making steady directional progress. Momentum signals are trusted more.

Range / Choppy

Price is bouncing with no clear direction. The model leans on support/resistance instead of momentum.

High volatility

Big, fast moves. Thresholds tighten so the model doesn't chase every spike.

Compression

Price is coiling in a tight range, often before a bigger move. The model watches for the breakout direction.

How a Kalshi 15-min BTC market works

Each contract covers one 15-minute window with a fixed strike price, set near BTC's price at the window's open.

The question

"Will BTC be above or below the strike when this window closes?" The model's job is estimating the real odds of that.

The price

Contracts trade 1¢–99¢. A price of 62¢ for YES roughly means the market thinks there's a 62% chance it settles YES.

Settlement

At the window's close, the contract pays out $1 if you were right, $0 if not. The last couple of minutes matter most.

Educational tool. Model output is a probability estimate, not a guarantee — Kalshi contracts can settle either way. Nothing here is financial advice.
